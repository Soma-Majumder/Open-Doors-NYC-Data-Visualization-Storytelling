About this data: these figures are national education research, not NYC's own school-report numbers used everywhere else on this page. The earnings estimates come from a long-term study of California students (Rose & Betts, The Review of Economics and Statistics); the STEM-completion figures are cited via The 74 and the Pioneer Institute. They show a strong association between advanced math coursework and later outcomes — not a guarantee for any individual student. Research is also clear this benefit depends on real support alongside access, not placement alone: a series of causal studies on the "Algebra for All" movement found that pushing students into accelerated math without adequate support increased failure rates for those who weren't ready, while Chicago's "double-dose" model (two class periods instead of one) improved both test scores and long-run graduation and college-enrollment rates.

Question for further investigation

The city grades outcomes. It has never graded access.

NYC DOE benchmarks whether a school's students perform well against similar schools — a "comparison group average" attached to most performance metrics.
